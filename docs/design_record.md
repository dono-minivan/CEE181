# Design Record (one evolving document)

## 1. Question and prediction
Is actuated freeway on-ramp metering better than pre-timed or no signal at all volumes? Prediction: actuated wins only in a middle band near capacity (X about 0.9 to 1.05). See Intermediate 1 for the full reasoning and "what would change our conclusion".

**Evaluation definitions (team decision 2026-11-15; set after E7, so they apply as fixed rules from E8 onward):**
- *Better.* A control mode is better than another at a demand level when its mean TTT over seeds 181-184 is at least 2% lower AND it is lower in at least 3 of 4 seed-paired runs. Any win is confirmed on held-out seeds 185-188. Why 2%: changing dt (0.05/0.1/0.2 s) moves TTT by < 1% (section 4), so 2% is above numerical noise.
- *Congested interval.* A 30-s interval at M_mrg (50 m upstream of the merge) is congested when its mean speed < v_c (computed IDM critical speed, 18.6 m/s = 41.5 mph) OR its occupancy > o_c, the critical occupancy o_c = k_c (l + L_loop) = 0.0261 veh/m x 7 m = 18.3% (computed in `tmc.report_figures.critical_occupancy`, reported as 18%). A congestion episode needs >= 2 consecutive congested intervals (60 s). o_c was chosen over a provisional data-based 15% (gap between free-flow 5-6% and congested 16-50% at M_mrg in E7) so the threshold traces to theory. Check: the E7 congested-minutes table is identical with 15% and 18% (`results/PR_congestion_minutes.csv`).

## 2. Model and assumptions
IDM, ballistic update, parameters in `configs/idm_baseline.yaml`. Limitations: deterministic driving (stochastic only in arrivals and CAV placement). Up to E7 drivers have no reaction time (ACC-like); from E9 on, humans have a reaction delay T_r = 0.6 s on car-following, restarted after every leader change in setting B (section 7).

Baseline simulator (`src/tmc/network.py`, single lane, no merge yet):
- Arrivals: piecewise-constant demand, Poisson (seeded) or uniform.
- Upstream boundary: point queue. A vehicle enters at x = 0 when the gap to the last vehicle is at least s0 + v_last*T, at speed v_last (v0 if the road is empty). Waiting vehicles are never dropped, and their wait counts in travel time.
- Downstream boundary: free exit. Exit time is interpolated within the step.
- On-ramp (`configs/scenario_s1_onramp.yaml`): ramp entrance (stop line, future meter site) -> 250 m approach -> 250 m acceleration lane (1500-1750 m). Ramp vehicles merge when the MOBIL safety criterion holds (gaps > 0, merging vehicle and new follower both decelerate no harder than b_safe = 4 m/s^2). If they cannot merge, they stop 1 m before the lane end. Optional switches: cooperative yielding, speed adaptation.
- **Merge model is PROVISIONAL.** With one mainline lane, the merge discharges only ~1430-1490 veh/h and breaks down even at X = 0.92 (experiment log E1 correction). Options: (a) two mainline lanes with MOBIL lane changing; (b) recalibrate the single-lane merge against a target merge capacity; (c) keep it and define X against the measured merge capacity instead of q_c. Team decision pending. *Resolved 2026-11-08 (E6, below): S3 merge + X_m.*
- Two mainline lanes (2026-11-07, option (a) above): `road.n_lanes`, symmetric MOBIL discretionary lane changes, ramp merges into lane 0 (right). Lane mechanics verified (section 4). **But the two-lane merge still deadlocks:** after ~600 s no ramp vehicle merges while the mainline runs at 61 mph (experiment log E2). The merge model, not the lane count, is the binding problem. Candidate fixes, team decision pending: (i) gap-seeking ramp behavior (the ramp vehicle adjusts speed toward the adjacent gap instead of matching the leader); (ii) anticipatory lane changes: lane-0 drivers approaching the merge zone count nearby ramp vehicles in their MOBIL incentive and move left; (iii) a forced/cooperative merge after waiting at the lane end (Hidas 2005 merge classes); (iv) a firmer lane-end trigger. Each is a modeling assumption and must be sourced and verified before control comparisons.
- 2026-11-07 (E3): fixes (i) and (ii) implemented as switches (`gap_seeking`, `anticipation_m`, off by default) and tested. The best combination reaches 88% of 2 q_c and the mainline still breaks down at X = 0.92. **Team decision: the two-lane pathway was expected to be one of the less viable options. Stop tuning it and move on to options (b) and (c).** The multi-lane code stays verified and available.
- 2026-11-07 (E4): measured the single-lane merge (option (c)). It acts as a give-way junction, not a bottleneck with one capacity: the mainline keeps priority and the ramp capacity c_r(q_m) falls from ~520 veh/h at q_m = 850 to ~0 at q_m = 1600. The no-queue threshold is ~1200 veh/h total (0.69 q_c). So (c) alone does not give a credible no-control baseline: the unmetered merge already starves the ramp, which a meter cannot improve on. **Recommendation: option (b), a merge that cannot shut the ramp out (forced/cooperative merge after a wait, Hidas 2005), with a target merge capacity near q_c and validation that the mainline breaks down above it.** Team decision pending.
- 2026-11-07 (E5, E5b): option (b) tried as a bounded zipper courtesy merge (`ramp.courtesy: zipper`, off by default, verified in tests/test_zipper.py). It ends ramp starvation, but the merge then becomes a stop-start point at the lane end: ~800 veh/h saturated, 0.46 q_c. Adding gap seeking lifts this to ~1160 veh/h (0.66 q_c), still below the pre-registered 0.85 q_c. **The fix did not meet its success criteria.** Decision pending on how to proceed (options in the experiment log, E5b).
- 2026-11-08 (E6, **team decision: option 1**): S3 (`configs/scenario_s3_onramp_zipgap.yaml`: zipper courtesy + gap seeking) is the no-control baseline. The demand ratio is redefined against the merge's measured saturated discharge: **X_m = (q_in + lambda_r) / C_m(r)**, with C_m(0.28) = 1157 veh/h (CV 4.4%, capacity drop ~6%, `results/S3_onramp_zipgap_nocontrol_merge_capacity.csv`). Control experiments hold the ramp share at r = 0.28, where all acceptance criteria hold. C_m = 0.66 q_c is a stated limitation of the single-lane IDM merge. The Intermediate 1 prediction band (X ~ 0.9-1.05) is restated in X_m.
- Derived values are computed in `src/tmc/idm.py`. Baseline q_c = 1742 veh/h at v_c = 18.6 m/s (41.5 mph); k_jam = 1/(s0+l) = 230 veh/mi.

## 5. Results
**E7 (2026-11-08), control-mode comparison, S3, r = 0.28, X_m against C_m = 1157 veh/h.** `results/E7_summary.csv`, `results/E7_ttt_vs_X.png`, run with `python -m tmc.evaluate`.
- Up to X_m = 1.10, no control is best. ALINEA (one setpoint, o_set = 0.20 at M_mrg) switches itself off and ties it. Pre-timed (best rate per level) costs +11 to +16% TTT.
- At X_m = 1.35, ALINEA cuts TTT by ~8% versus no control in 8 of 8 seeds (4 used for tuning, 4 held out), with the ramp queue <= 23 < n_max = 35. At X_m = 1.20 the effect is small and inconsistent.
- Answer to the Intermediate 1 question in this model: actuated metering is not better at all demand levels, and it does not win in the predicted middle band. It wins only under heavy overload. Pre-timed metering never beats no control here, most likely because standing-start releases merge below mainline speed (experiment log E7).
- Detector-based estimation and forecasting work as intended: the queue estimate is within 2 veh of truth, and the ramp-demand forecast beats persistence by ~19% MAE.

## 7. Final pathway decision (CAV or signal)
**CAV control (team, 2026-11-23).** Ramp metering stays as the shared TMC foundation; the final pathway is vehicle-level CAV control on the same freeway segment. Spec revised 2026-11-23 after review, before any code or runs (see experiment log E9).

**Design question (confirmed, team 2026-11-23).** In mixed traffic, does sharing the leader's acceleration by V2V (CACC) reduce how much braking disturbances grow, compared with the same controller using only its own sensors (ACC) and with human drivers, while keeping throughput and total travel time and without unsafe gaps or hard braking?

**Design (team decisions 2026-11-23):**
- *Human baseline:* IDM with a reaction delay T_r = 0.6 s applied to all inputs, a(t) = a_IDM(s(t - T_r), v(t - T_r), v_l(t - T_r)) (Treiber & Kesting 2013, Sec. 12.2; T_r from Table 12.1). A switch, `reaction_time_s`, default 0 so E0-E7 reproduce exactly. No estimation errors, noise, temporal or multi-vehicle anticipation. The delay does not change the steady state (q_c, v_c, fundamental diagram). Every human (p = 0) run is redone with the switch on.
  - *Implementation (2026-11-23, `src/tmc/network.py`, YAML `drivers: reaction_time_s`):* a ring buffer keeps each vehicle's (x, v) for the last T_r. **Team decisions 2026-11-23:** (1) the delay applies to car-following only (mainline lanes and the ramp lane's IDM toward its leader); merge safety, zipper courtesy, yielding, lane-end braking, gap seeking and MOBIL stay undelayed (stated limitation, so the merge model validated in E6 changes only through car-following); (2) after a leader change (merge, cut-in), the follower uses the new leader's stored state from T_r ago (all lanes share the mainline position coordinate), falling back to current values when the new leader has less than T_r of history or its delayed gap is <= 0 (it was behind the follower T_r ago); (3) a vehicle with less than T_r of history (just entered, incl. metered standing starts) uses current values. A scripted-lead hook (`lead_accel`, vehicle 0) was added for verification and E9 setting A.
  - *Leader-change rule (team decision 2026-11-25, experiment log E9e):* option `leader_change_delay: restart` (S3r2, used by setting B): for T_r after any leader change the follower uses current inputs. It replaces rule (2) above for merges and cut-ins, where a just-merged vehicle's delayed gap to its new leader (from when the two were side by side) was positive but tiny and produced decelerations of 1e3-1e5 m/s^2. Setting A (no leader changes) is unaffected. Cost: humans react to a cut-in without delay (optimistic for humans, so conservative for a CAV benefit). S3r (E9b, C_m 978) is superseded for setting B by S3r2 (E9b2).
- *Controller:* textbook ACC model (Treiber & Kesting 2013, Sec. 11.3.8, Eq. 11.26): CAH blended with the car-following acceleration, coolness c = 0.99, effective leader acceleration min(a_l, a). **Base term = IDM, not the book's IIDM (design choice, team 2026-11-23).** Reason: with an IDM base, human, ACC and CACC vehicles share the same steady state, and the CAH term is zero there, so only the control law differs. IIDM for CAVs only would hold gap s0 + vT exactly and raise capacity from 1742 to 1989 veh/h (+14.1%, computed by `tmc.idm.iidm_capacity`; the IIDM maximum is the limit v -> v0), which would mix a steady-state change into the comparison (the guide rules out a parameter change alone). IIDM for everyone would invalidate q_c, C_m, X_m and E0-E7. Caveat: this departs from the book's (IIDM-based) ACC model but matches the original published version, Kesting, Treiber & Helbing 2010 (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 368, Eq. 2.4 on a plain IDM base; checked 2026-11-26); Eq. 11.26 blends with any base acceleration, and the book states the ACC model should behave like the IDM in most situations. Optional extension: CAVs on IIDM as an ablation. Same T, s0, a, b, v0 for all vehicle types.
- *ACC vs CACC differ only in the leader-acceleration input a_l:*
  - ACC: estimated from its own sensors. Leader speed = own speed - sensed approach rate, sampled every 0.1 s, differenced, then a first-order low-pass filter, T_f = 0.3 s (design choice; the book's footnote to Eq. 11.26 warns that differencing needs care). ACC therefore sees the leader's *realized* motion, late by the leader's actuator lag plus sampling plus filter.
  - CACC: the leader broadcasts its *commanded* acceleration (controller output before its actuator lag) every 0.1 s, used as feed-forward as in Ploeg et al. 2011 (Eq. 7, Sec. III-A). *Message timing:* messages are sent on the 0.1 s grid and become usable at the first 0.1 s tick at or after send time + delay. All ages are counted in integer ticks (delay ticks = ceil(round(delay / 0.1 s, 9)), computed once; the rounding stops a value such as 0.3 / 0.1 = 2.9999... or a hair above an integer from changing the tick count), so no floating-point age comparison is made and the effective delay is the same at every dt. Nominal delay 0.15 s (measured in Ploeg et al. 2011, Sec. V-B) is therefore usable 2 ticks (0.2 s) after sending; each lost message adds 1 tick of age. *Update order:* at each 0.1 s tick, controller commands are computed leader-first (downstream to upstream along each lane), so a 0-tick delay means the follower uses its leader's command from the same tick, and results do not depend on vehicle array order (design choice, 2026-11-23).
  - Fallback to the ACC estimate when the leader is not equipped (humans do not broadcast), when there is no message yet from the current leader (the link restarts after any cut-in or leader change), or when the newest usable message is more than 5 ticks (0.5 s) old, compared as integers (design choice). Every fallback is logged with its reason.
- *Command to motion:* command clipped to [-8 m/s^2, a] (book Fig. 11.9 limit); realized acceleration follows a first-order lag, tau = 0.1 s nominal (identified for real test vehicles, Ploeg et al. 2011, Sec. V-A); tau = 0.5 s as a sensitivity case for a slower vehicle (design choice).
- *Sampling and time step:* sensing and messages every 0.1 s, a fixed controller property; dt must divide 0.1 s exactly. CAV dt check at 0.025 / 0.05 / 0.1 s. Human-only check with T_r on at 0.05 / 0.1 / 0.2 s (T_r / dt must be an integer).
- *What each comparison isolates:* human vs ACC changes three things at once (reaction delay, CAH term, actuator lag), so it is reported as "human vs automated driving", not as one mechanism. CACC vs ACC is the clean test of communication.
- *Merge (setting B):* equipped vehicles use their own ACC acceleration in the MOBIL safety check and the zipper courtesy test, with the same yielding rules as humans (courtesy at most once, same threshold) and no cooperative-merging logic. Ramp vehicles are equipped with probability p.
  - *Implementation (2026-11-23, `src/tmc/network.py`, config `cav:`):* equipped vehicles drawn once per vehicle ID with their own RNG (`placement_seed`), so arrivals are unchanged; controller updates every 0.1 s tick, leader-first along each lane, each command broadcast right after it is computed; the realized (lagged) acceleration replaces the car-following term of an equipped vehicle (no reaction delay), and the other demands (lane end, gap seeking, courtesy, yielding) are applied on top as a min, as for humans. In merge safety, courtesy and yielding checks an equipped vehicle's acceleration toward the NEW leader is its ACC command with a_l missing (no track yet), which by the confirmed missing-a_l rule equals the IDM clipped to [-8, a]; since the checks compare against -b_safe = -4 m/s^2, **equipped and human merge decisions are numerically identical**. Consequence for E9 prediction 4: the predicted mechanism "equipped followers pass the merge safety check more often" cannot operate at the decision itself; any merge effect comes from how equipped vehicles drive after a cut-in (noted before any setting B run). Fallbacks are logged per tick in `SimResult.cav_events`. **Stated limitation (team, 2026-11-23):** CAV merge decisions are not modeled separately from human ones (no cooperative or CAV-specific gap acceptance), so setting B cannot show a CAV merge-decision benefit; benefits there are likely understated, not overstated. Prediction 4 was amended accordingly before any setting B run (experiment log E9).
- *Penetration:* p = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0; equipped vehicles assigned at random by seed.
- *Settings:* (A) single-lane platoon disturbance; (B) S3 merge at high demand. Pre-registered in experiment log E9.

**Acceptance criteria (fixed before E9 runs):**
- *Verification gates (before interpreting):* p = 0 with T_r = 0 is bit-identical to the current baseline; CACC with communication off is bit-identical to ACC; equipped vehicles hold the IDM equilibrium gap in steady state (q_c unchanged); controller reads only permitted inputs (tested like `DetectorView`); gap > 0 in every run; conservation; metrics change < 2% across the dt sets above; every fallback logged. Humans with T_r = 0.6 s must be collision-free; if not, add temporal anticipation (book Eqs. 12.13-12.14) and record the change.
- *Main metric:* growth rate g per vehicle, the slope of ln(dv_i) against vehicle index i, where dv_i = v_eq - min v_i, fitted over vehicles i = 0 (lead) to 30 with dv_i > 0.01 m/s and min v_i > 0.5 m/s (did not stop). At least 5 vehicles must qualify; otherwise g is reported as "not estimable" with the reason (wave decayed below 0.01 m/s, or too many vehicles stopped), and that seed is judged on A instead (A at least 10% lower counts as the 10% criterion). g > 0 means the wave grows. The number of stopped vehicles is reported separately. The amplification A (last follower / lead) and the deficit at vehicle 10 are secondary.
- *Disturbance reduced* (judged on the **nominal** setting A disturbance and on setting B; team decision 2026-11-23 after E9a, before any CAV run) at a penetration p: g is lower than human and lower than ACC by at least 0.0035 per vehicle (= a 10% smaller amplification over 30 vehicles, ln(1/0.9)/30) in >= 3 of 4 placement seeds (181-184), confirmed on held-out seeds 185-188.
- *Throughput preserved:* throughput and TTT within 2% of human, runs continued until queues clear, unmet demand reported.
- *Gaps acceptable:* min net gap >= 1 m for every follower at all speeds; min time gap >= 0.5 s, evaluated only while v > 5 m/s; max deceleration <= 4 m/s^2 (= b_safe of the merge model) required of equipped vehicles. For humans, hard-braking counts are reported as a finding, not a pass/fail. Gaps of humans following CAVs are reported too.
- *Robust communication:* at 0.3 s message delay, CACC is no worse than ACC (g within 0.0035, TTT within 2%) and meets the gap criteria. At 1.0 s delay and during a 5 s outage, CACC falls back (logged) and matches ACC, and it recovers after the outage. Otherwise that is the reported failure condition.
- *Strong disturbance = stress case (team decision 2026-11-23, E9a):* its human g (0.0052 with T_r) leaves too little margin to test growth, so it is not used for the "disturbance reduced" criterion. It is reported for every case (g, A, stopped count, min speed) and the gap and deceleration criteria apply to it in full.
- *Adoption supported* only if all of the above hold at p = 0.5 and p = 1.0. A supported negative result is acceptable.

**Setting A result (2026-11-24, experiment log E9c):** CACC is indistinguishable from ACC at every penetration, delay and actuator lag (|g_CACC - g_ACC| <= 2e-5/veh against a 0.0035 margin), so "disturbance reduced" fails and CACC adoption is not supported in setting A. Gaps and robust-communication criteria pass. Fully automated traffic (ACC or CACC, p = 1) cuts g from 0.0119 to 0.0082, about what removing the human reaction delay gives (0.0076 in E9a); with tau = 0.5 s that gain disappears (0.0122). Mechanism: Eq. 11.26 equals a_IDM - c b x^3/3 + O(x^5) with x = (a_IDM - a_CAH)/b, so the leader acceleration enters only at third order outside critical situations. Figures: `results/E9A_matrix_g_vs_p.png`, `..._deficits.png`, `..._delay_sweep.png`.

**Extension E9d (2026-11-24, pre-registered; positive control):** the Ploeg et al. 2011 CACC (Eq. 8), which uses the leader's command linearly, in setting A only. At the IDM gap (acts like h = 1.79 s) V2V and a sensor estimate give the same result (g -0.020 vs -0.019 at p = 1, waves decay); at a short gap (h = 1.0 s, chosen from Eq. 17 before running) V2V beats the sensor estimate by 0.0047/veh (> 0.0035, criterion passes) and the no-feed-forward version collides. The delay sweep crosses the sensor level between 0.3 and 0.5 s, as the theory says (0.32 s). Conclusion of E9c + E9d: V2V helps only with a control law that uses the leader's acceleration linearly AND a gap short enough that sensing is not enough; at human-like gaps the control law matters, not the communication. The short gap changes the steady state, so it is not a throughput comparison. Setting B is unchanged (Eq. 11.26). Figures: `results/E9D_g_by_feedforward.png`, `E9D_deficits.png`, `E9D_delay_sweep.png`.

**Setting B result (2026-11-25, experiment log E9e; S3r2 merge, C_m 1069 veh/h (E9b2), dt 0.05 s, seeds 181-184 + held-out 185-188):** automated driving cuts TTT by 15-27% at X 1.35 at every penetration, confirmed on held-out seeds, with higher merge discharge (1092 -> up to 1321 veh/h) and unchanged throughput; at X 1.20 the gain depends on whether the human run breaks down (-2.6% in-sample, -21% held-out at p = 1). CACC never beats ACC reproducibly; D1 (congested minutes) fails everywhere; at p = 1 ACC tends to be better (confirmed at X 1.20, reversed on held-out seeds at X 1.35), although CACC's leader-acceleration input is more accurate. Two implementation findings: the reaction-delay stale-gap artifact at leader changes (fixed: `leader_change_delay: restart`), and the V2V message content (broadcasting u instead of the effective command min(u, other demands) let ramp vehicles announce acceleration while braking; fixed as `broadcast: effective`, as-run result kept). Gaps stay at the merge-model floor (~0.79 m) for humans and CAVs alike. Verdict: CACC adoption not supported in setting B either; the benefit at the merge comes from automation (no reaction delay), not from communication. Figures: `results/E9B_ttt_vs_p.png`, `E9B_congestion_vs_p.png`.
